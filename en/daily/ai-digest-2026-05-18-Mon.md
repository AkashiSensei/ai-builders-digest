## Podcast

### No Priors: Pax Silica: Inside the Trump Administration's Tech Strategy with US Under Secretary of State for Economic Affairs Jacob Helberg

The takeaway: Jacob Helberg is trying to turn AI supply chain security into a product-like platform run with private companies, not a government-operated industrial plan.

Helberg, the US Under Secretary of State for Economic Affairs, describes Pax Silica as a 14-country economic security coalition focused on the full AI supply chain. The first major rollout is a 4,000-acre economic security zone in the Philippines, currently held as diplomatic property, with a two-year window to negotiate investor protections, tax treatment, and long-term operating rules. The target is not only chips. He names precision reducers, servo motors, rare earth magnets, actuators, robotics supply chains, minerals, logistics, and energy as the real surface area of AI industrial capacity.

The sharpest contrast is with China's Belt and Road model. Helberg argues the US should not build state-owned supply chains or government-run infrastructure abroad. Instead, the American edge is private-sector execution: platforms that are commercially viable, positive-sum for host countries, and eventually able to live outside government as durable private services. His memorable line is that "we're not gonna do government operated supply chains because that's not how we shine as a country."

The practical ask for builders and investors is feedback and execution judgment. Venture capital can help identify which founders can actually execute in capital-intensive, strategically important domains; companies can tell policymakers where market access, supply-chain partnerships, IP protection, model distillation, mineral pricing, and energy constraints are blocking deployment.
