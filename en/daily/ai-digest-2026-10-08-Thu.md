## Podcast

### AI & I by Every: Why Every Traded Personal Agents for One Company Agent

The Takeaway: A single shared company agent beats a fleet of personal ones, because the real leverage comes from everyone contributing to, and drawing on, one accumulated body of context.

Willie Williams, head of platform at Every, has spent the past year watching his company cycle through agent setups, and his conclusion is that most people do not want to maintain their own. Every started with personal agents built on OpenClaw, and for a while they were beloved: people wired them into family group chats and gave them names. The problem was that they were technical, fragile, and hard to secure, so "if you don't invest in it, it fades." The shift came from a simple reframe: "instead of everyone investing in their own agents and making them better day to day over time... why don't we take all that energy and put it into one agent that the whole company benefits from?"

Williams is specific about what breaks in the personal-agent model. Naming is a tax, context is fragmented, and a newly onboarded agent lacks the company's history. A single agent, by contrast, learns the workflows and gets better as everyone works with it. He draws a sharp line between work and home: on the professional side he expects "one agent per company or org structure," while at home personalization wins and people will happily keep an agent that is just theirs.

The most counterintuitive point is about management. Williams says engineering management "feels the most similar out of any of the jobs" he has done, because the job is still "a people job." His leverage increased rather than vanished: the valuable work now is finding bottlenecks, and much of the mechanical work of tracking team health, alerts, and on-call rotation can be routed to an agent. He uses the open-source app Tend to consolidate meetings and notes into a single feed, giving him "a higher field marshal level view" of the company. But when it comes to action, "we still need to sit down and have a conversation."
