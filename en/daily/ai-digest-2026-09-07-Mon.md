## Podcast

### No Priors: Rethinking Legacy Data Infrastructure with Eon Co-Founders Ofir Ehrlich and Gonen Stein

The Takeaway: Data, not models, is becoming the moat of the AI era, and the winners will be enterprises that unlock and secure the data they already own.

Ofir Ehrlich and Gonen Stein co-founded Eon, a cloud data foundation with backup and disaster recovery at its core, built for the AI era; the pair previously built CloudEndure, which AWS acquired. Their central argument: models, compute, and infrastructure are becoming commodities with almost zero switching costs, so a company's accumulated data is what truly differentiates it. The evidence, they say, is everywhere: Google paid $10 million for the data of bankrupt Spirit Airlines, not its airplanes, with other AI companies also bidding, and AI labs are approaching hedge funds to buy data that once sat on tapes collecting dust.

Their contrarian point: most companies already own the data they need; it is just locked up across business units, half-understood systems, and expensive formats. Eon maps and classifies data across environments, masks sensitive fields, ingests structured and unstructured sources, and connects them to LLM workflows without custom pipelines per application. "Customers already have this data," they say. "It's locked. It's not accessible."

They also argue the threat model has shifted from humans to agents. At AWS they saw a large customer lose 60% of its environment to ransomware because resources were never mapped, classified, and tagged; now the same class of risk comes from AI agents with legitimate access and permissions, moving at extreme velocity. Their advice: "We need to assume breach, whether it's malicious or not." Six months ago few leaders would discuss it; now almost every leader either fears it or has lived it, while nontechnical employees build with tools like Lovable and handle sensitive company data outside any governance.

Expect more dashboards, not fewer: "We'll see more dashboards because this will be the only way to figure out what the hell is going on in the world," as agents write most code, agents activate other agents, and non-human identities proliferate. Compared with the cloud era, this transformation is faster, and companies are losing control to the point where fear becomes an inhibitor. Most companies, they say, are still at the beginning.
