## Podcast

### AI & I by Every: How Sam Altman Uses Dots to Take Back His Time

The Takeaway: The Dots are less a faster chat window than a bid to return the attention always-on work quietly consumes, and OpenAI is betting the next platform shift on agents that act in the background rather than wait for prompts.

Sam Altman, the CEO of OpenAI, describes the Dots launched at Dev Day as the first thing that has given him back control of his mornings and his mental space. He used to love the early mornings, his most creative hours, for the day's deepest project. Running a large company changed that: overnight problems could become real disasters if ignored for an hour, and reaching for his phone first thing polluted his brain for the rest of the day. Now he trusts his dot to judge what actually needs his attention, and says it gave him back creative space and time with his kids.

The bigger claim is that this is a platform shift. Altman describes "this world of sort of persistent intelligence, active intelligence," with agents "running in the background 24/7, connected to your systems, doing stuff proactively for you," and says OpenAI is opening its ecosystem so people can build apps inside ChatGPT, bring their subscription wherever they like, and use a marketplace. The key unlock, he says, was Astra, a model smart enough to clear the intelligence bar.

The examples are specific. A dot messaged him five minutes before a Dev Day keynote demo warning the stage setup would break it, and offered to fix it. Another caught one thing he had to handle in a day of back-to-back meetings that he never would have found otherwise. When a Codex search failed to find something he had seen in Slack, his dot kept looking overnight and found it: a screenshot he had misremembered as text. He has also woken to find a feature built from voice notes he left while drifting off.

On speed, Altman says he now does all his prompting on Ultrafast and cites designer Bret Victor's principle that creators should have an immediate connection to what they are creating. OpenAI spent years driving down the price of intelligence; speed is the next axis, with its own chip, roughly 8x faster inference at a good price soon and 100x beyond. He frames the stakes as Renaissance versus Industrial Revolution, and wants a wave of discovery and company formation with people, not machines, at the center.
