### AI & I by Every: A $10B Hedge Fund's AI Playbook (Best of the Pod)

The Takeaway: A hedge fund managing close to $10 billion can rewire its entire organization around AI when the owner-operator treats fluency as a firm-wide requirement rather than an option.

核心要点：一家管理近 100 亿美元资产的对冲基金，只要老板兼经营者（owner-operator）把 AI 熟练度变成全员硬指标，整家公司就能围绕 AI 快速转型。

Will is CEO, CIO and managing partner of Walleye, a hedge fund with about 400 people. Two years ago, an analyst on one of its long-short stock-picking teams used early GPT-3-era tools to become dramatically more efficient, eventually aiming to replace his own analyst work. Will was skeptical until he saw the demo. That effort grew into Current, an internal AI product that digests analyst notes, broker research, PDFs and earnings transcripts, and over 50 outside firms have asked to beta-test it.

Will 是 Walleye 的 CEO、CIO 兼管理合伙人，这家对冲基金约有 400 名员工。两年前，他旗下一支股票多空团队的分析师开始用早期的 GPT-3 工具让自己变得高效得多，最终目标是取代自己的分析师工作。Will 起初并不相信，直到看了演示才被说服；这个项目后来成长为 Current，也就是 Walleye 内部用来消化分析师笔记、券商资料、PDF 和财报电话会议记录的 AI 产品，目前已有超过 50 家外部机构申请试用。

Will then sent the entire firm a memo, "AI at Walleye: a challenge to all of us": "I use ChatGPT to write this email. You should be using it too and be proud of it. As a hedge fund, we should be ashamed to leave money on the table by ignoring tools that make us faster, smarter, and more effective." His blunt framing: "Not using these tools is like refusing to use the Internet in 1995 because it wasn't perfect."

他给全公司发了一封题为「AI at Walleye: a challenge to all of us」的邮件：「我用 ChatGPT 写了这封邮件，你们也应该用起来，并且为此感到自豪。作为一家对冲基金，放着能让我们更快、更聪明、更高效的工具不用，把钱留在桌上，是应该感到羞耻的。」他的比喻更直白：「不用这些工具，就像 1995 年因为互联网还不完美就拒绝使用它一样。」

The words are backed by machinery. Base-level AI proficiency is mandatory for every employee, supported by weekly meetups, usage leaderboards, rewards for suggesting tools adopted firm-wide, and an "AI Senate." Will writes the concepts as bullet points and has an LLM write them back in his own voice, cutting email and memo work from four or five hours to about fifteen minutes. "These tools don't negate the necessity to think," he says. "If anything, they should just give you more time to think."

这些口号背后有整套机制支撑：每位员工都必须达到基础的 AI 熟练度，公司用每周分享会、工具使用排行榜、推荐工具被全公司采纳后的奖励，以及一个「AI Senate」来推动这件事。Will 说自己现在先写出要点，再让 LLM 用他本人的语气成文，过去要花四五个小时的邮件和长文，现在大约十五分钟就能完成。「这些工具并没有取消思考的必要，」他说，「如果有的话，它们只是给了你更多时间去思考。」

He is matter-of-fact about the endgame: Walleye records nearly every Zoom and call, runs LLMs over risk-meeting transcripts, and expects organizations to become "collectives" of connected information. His prediction for knowledge workers: "Even if you're an individual and you don't manage any human, you're still gonna be managing employees. A lot of those employees are just going to be AI robots." His contrarian investing bet: humans still hold an edge in the fuzzy mess where machines have not seen all the priors, and these tools should make those humans' predictions more accurate.

他对终局同样直言不讳：Walleye 几乎录下每一场 Zoom 和电话，用 LLM 处理风控会议的文字记录，并预计公司会变成把所有信息连接起来的「collective」（信息集合体）。对每一位知识工作者，他的预判是：「即使你只是个人贡献者、手底下没有人类员工，你依然要管理员工，而其中很多员工会是 AI 机器人。」不过作为投资人，他的反直觉观点是：在机器还没见过足够多先例的「模糊地带」，人类依然有优势，而这些工具应该让人类的预测变得更准。
