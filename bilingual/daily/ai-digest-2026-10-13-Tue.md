### AI & I by Every: How Sam Altman Uses Dots to Take Back His Time

The Takeaway: The most concrete lesson from OpenAI CEO Sam Altman's use of Dots is not about automation but about attention, because a trusted agent that separates the urgent from the noise hands back the deep, creative hours that constant checking destroys.

核心要点：OpenAI CEO Sam Altman 使用 Dots 最实在的收获，与其说是自动化，不如说是注意力。一个值得信任的 agent 能替他分清什么是紧急、什么是噪音，把被反复查看手机毁掉的大块创造性时间还给他。

Sam Altman, the CEO of OpenAI, describes Dots, the assistant his company launched at Dev Day, as "the first thing that has given me back control of my mornings and my mental space in the mornings." The trap was familiar: he loved the early hours for deep work, but running a big company means something breaks overnight, so reaching for his phone "polluted my brain with all these other things" before he could do his most creative work. His dot now triages what genuinely needs him and handles the rest, and he trusts it to "have good judgment about what I need to think about, not think about."

Sam Altman 是 OpenAI 的 CEO。他把公司在 Dev Day 发布的助手 Dots 形容为「the first thing that has given me back control of my mornings and my mental space in the mornings」。过去的陷阱很熟悉：他喜欢清晨那段深度工作时光，但经营一家大公司意味着夜里总有事出岔子，于是一醒来就摸手机，在还没开始最需要创造力的工作前就「polluted my brain with all these other things」。现在他的 dot 会先判断哪些真正需要他、其余替他处理，他信任它「have good judgment about what I need to think about, not think about」。

The payoff shows up in small, specific saves. His dot once caught a scheduling conflict that was never on his calendar because it had read it in Slack. It once found, overnight, a screenshot in Slack that he had spent ten or twenty minutes hunting with Codex and then abandoned. And in a task he says he could not have hired anyone to do, he left raw notes and voice memos as ideas struck, woke to five or six built versions he could iterate on, and on another occasion went to sleep after a long voice note only for the work, plus "the next seven steps," to be done by morning. "I would have given up on that," he says of the Slack search. "It just sat there very patiently looking for something to do to be helpful."

回报体现在一个个具体的小救场里。他的 dot 曾发现一个日程冲突，而那个冲突根本不在日历上，因为它是从 Slack 里读出来的。它也曾在夜里找回一张 Slack 里的截图，那是他用 Codex 搜了十几二十分钟后放弃的东西。还有一类任务，他说自己根本没法靠雇人完成：他把灵感随手记成文字与语音备忘，一觉醒来就有了五六个可迭代的版本；另一次他留下一段长长的语音备忘就睡了，第二天早上事情不仅做完了，还顺手往下推进了「the next seven steps」。「I would have given up on that」，他谈到那次 Slack 搜索时说，「It just sat there very patiently looking for something to do to be helpful.」

Altman frames all of it as a Renaissance rather than just an Industrial Revolution. The Renaissance, he argues, was centered on people; the Industrial Revolution on machines that often turned workers into "a cog in a machine." He rejects the darker vision in which humans become "house cats" whose rewards machines decide, and wants the AI wave to expand human potential instead. That optimism has teeth: if the tools really make people more productive, then "we better build more ourselves," and "there are way more good ideas outside of our building than inside." On product, he points to opening ChatGPT so anyone can build apps and bring their subscription, and to a new speed axis in Ultrafast. The strategy underneath is to keep pushing value per dollar so hard it "put Moore's law to shame," while adding speed as a fresh dimension and investing in the company's own chip so fast inference reaches everyone. The mechanic he keeps returning to is simple: "if you lower friction a little bit, people build way more."

Altman 把这一切框定成一次文艺复兴，而不只是工业革命。他认为文艺复兴以人为中心，工业革命则围绕机器，常常把工人变成「a cog in a machine」。他拒绝那种人类终将沦为「house cats」、由机器决定奖赏的悲观剧本，希望这波 AI 浪潮扩展人的潜能。这种乐观是有分量的：如果工具真能让人更高产，那么「we better build more ourselves」，而且「there are way more good ideas outside of our building than inside」。在产品上，他指向把 ChatGPT 开放给所有人构建应用、并允许带上自己的订阅，也指向 Ultrafast 带来的速度新维度。底层策略是持续把每一美元的价值推高到「put Moore's law to shame」，同时引入速度这一新维度，并投入自研芯片，让快速推理惠及所有人。他反复回到的那条朴素机制是：「if you lower friction a little bit, people build way more.」
